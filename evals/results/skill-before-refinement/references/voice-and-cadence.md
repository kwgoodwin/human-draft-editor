# Voice and cadence

Use when the user supplies writing samples, names voice preferences, or requests an author-specific edit.

## A small, optional voice brief

Extract only observable preferences relevant to the task: formality, first-person use, sentence rhythm, vocabulary and technical terminology, punctuation, and level of directness. A sentence or two is usually enough. Apply explicit user preferences over inferences from samples; do not turn a sample's accidental feature into a rule.

Treat samples as evidence of style, not a source of facts to transplant. A sample about an author's children, travel or professional work does not authorize adding those experiences to another piece. First-person narration must be supported by the draft or separately supplied author facts intended for this piece. Do not replace an unfamiliar but deliberate voice with generic friendliness.

## A practical reading pass

Read each paragraph's first sentence in sequence. If several perform the same job, identify which repetition is dispensable and which qualifies a separate claim. Improve joins by using the existing logical relationship rather than adding stock bridges. Keep varied lengths where they serve the argument, not to satisfy a numerical rhythm target.

A necessary repeated term is often better than a fluent but imprecise synonym. A well-placed list is often better than a long sentence. The desired outcome is a coherent piece in the author's supported voice, not a piece with fewer supposedly AI-associated words.
