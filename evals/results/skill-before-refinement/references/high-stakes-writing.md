# Editing consequential claims

Use for legal, regulatory, scientific, medical, financial or technical claims where a style edit could materially change meaning. This is a preservation checklist, not a requirement to conduct a new investigation for every edit.

- Keep allegations, proposals, permissions, findings and final outcomes distinct. Accepting an argument for consideration is not adopting it.
- Preserve who bears a duty or burden, the population or jurisdiction covered, triggering conditions, exceptions, dates and the difference between discretionary and mandatory language.
- Keep measurements attached to their units, denominators, comparison groups, time windows and uncertainty. Do not turn a ceiling into an expected result or an association into causation.
- Keep each citation attached to the claim it actually supports. Do not merge differently sourced claims into one apparently supported statement.
- Headlines, decks, excerpts and social copy can circulate independently. Keep each necessary qualification in the component that needs it, even if this creates appropriate repetition across components.
- Do not strengthen a claim merely because background material supports a stronger one. A substantive correction needs authorization and an adequate source basis; otherwise preserve and flag it in the review notes when notes are allowed.
- If substantive correction is authorized, identify the correction and source basis separately from style edits. Do not invent citations or describe uninspected sources as verified.

Meaning preservation does not establish legal validity, medical safety, financial accuracy or publication readiness. Respect whatever separate review the user or publishing workflow actually requires; do not impose a particular tool, model or local process on every user.
